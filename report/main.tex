\begin{abstract}
\noindent Information on Tunisian administrative procedures is spread across ministry pages, the National
Business Register (RNE) site and Official Journal PDFs, in French and Arabic, while many citizens write in
Tunisian Derja. Large language models answer such questions fluently but may invent fees, deadlines, legal
provisions or required documents. We present a fully local assistant that answers in English, French, Arabic
and Derja (Arabic and Latin script) exclusively from 12 indexed official documents. An n8n workflow
orchestrates six specialised agents: a rule-first intent router with dialogue state, a hybrid retriever
(BGE-M3 dense retrieval, BM25, reciprocal rank fusion and exact procedure-code lookup), a checklist builder
that quotes official sections verbatim, a grounded-answer agent that quotes located legal articles and
otherwise generates citation- and number-verified answers, an OCR-based form checker and a dossier validator.
Hybrid retrieval raises Hit@5 from 0.704 to 0.873 on 84 development questions but only ties dense retrieval
(0.750) on 20 held-out questions. An independent audit of 728 end-to-end cases exposed source-only replies
for every contextual RNE question and a fabricated legal provision; after targeted corrections the final
candidate answers all 380 RNE table cases with the exact official row, retains every required official
span in 480/480 targeted checks and quotes the requested article instead of paraphrasing it.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} retrieval-augmented generation, multilingual retrieval, Tunisian Arabic, multi-agent orchestration, n8n, hallucination mitigation, document analysis.

\section{Introduction and problem definition}
Obtaining a passport or registering a company in Tunisia requires combining several official sources: the
Ministry of the Interior publishes procedure guides in French and Arabic \cite{interior_passport}; the RNE
publishes a table of 95 procedures with fees and legal deadlines \cite{rne_formalities} and the legal texts
that govern them \cite{law2018_52,decree2022_298}. These sources are fragmented, not always consistent (the
French and Arabic passport pages state different stamp duties, 20/60 vs.\ 25/80 TND) and written in languages
other than the one many users speak (Derja, often typed in Latin script with digits, e.g.\ \emph{``9adech
ya5ou wa9t el passbor?''}). A general chatbot is a risky answer: hallucinated requirements or amounts
\cite{ji2023hallucination} cost citizens time and money.

\textbf{Objectives.} (i) Answer only from verifiable official documents, with citations; (ii) refuse or ask
for clarification when the corpus cannot answer; (iii) support four query languages without altering official
text; (iv) check official forms and dossiers for missing information without certifying them; (v) run on one
laptop (4\,GB GPU) without paid APIs or exposure of personal data. \textbf{Scope:} passports, the national
identity card (CIN) and RNE business registration. Birth certificates are \emph{deliberately not covered}: the
only source found opts out of AI use in its \code{robots.txt}, so the procedure is reported as uncovered.

\section{System requirements and technology selection}
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the system as packaged with Docker Compose (all ports bound to \code{127.0.0.1}).
Each tool was chosen against a requirement rather than by default:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Python + FastAPI} \cite{fastapi} for the agents: typed request/response models (Pydantic) give
  every agent an explicit contract that n8n, tests and the Telegram bridge share; the extraction, OCR and
  embedding ecosystems are Python-native.
\item \textbf{n8n} \cite{n8n} as orchestrator: the agent chain is a visible, editable workflow with
  conditional branches and per-node error outputs, and it schedules re-ingestion. A hand-written Python
  pipeline would be simpler to test but opaque to non-programmers maintaining the service; an autonomous
  agent framework \cite{wu2023autogen} would add non-deterministic control flow, which a citation-bound
  administrative domain does not need.
\item \textbf{Qdrant} \cite{qdrant}: payload filters on procedure category and document, exact scroll by
  payload (used for RNE codes) and full provenance stored with each vector.
\item \textbf{Ollama} \cite{ollama}: local inference keeps questions and uploaded documents on the machine
  and costs nothing per query; quantised models fit 4\,GB of VRAM.
\item \textbf{Telegram} \cite{telegram_bot_api} as primary channel (long polling: no public URL or inbound
  port); a local web page and a CLI send the same requests to the same webhook as fallbacks.
\item \textbf{PyMuPDF, Tesseract} \cite{smith2007tesseract} \textbf{and OpenCV} for PDF text layers, Arabic and
  French OCR and geometric alignment of photographed forms.
\item \textbf{RAG instead of fine-tuning} \cite{lewis2020rag}: answers must stay traceable to a source and
  updatable when a decree changes; fine-tuning a 7B model is not feasible on this hardware.
\end{itemize}

\section{LLM selection and deployment constraints}
All models run in Ollama on an RTX 3050 laptop GPU (4\,GB VRAM) with 4-bit quantisation (Q4\_K\_M):
\textbf{BGE-M3} \cite{chen2024bgem3} (567M parameters, F16) for multilingual dense embeddings (1024-d), chosen
because one model embeds French, Arabic, English and Derja queries into the same space as French and Arabic
documents; \textbf{Mistral~7B Instruct} \cite{jiang2023mistral} for the three generative roles (routing
fallback, grounded answers, dialogue fact extraction), chosen as the strongest instruction-following model
that fits the GPU partially (about 40\,\% of its layers on GPU; the rest on CPU); and \textbf{TranslateGemma
4B} for French$\rightarrow$English translation of quoted checklist items. No paid API is used and no data
leaves the machine. All prompts use temperature~0; structured outputs use JSON mode or a JSON schema.

\textbf{Design consequences.} The 7B model is slow (20--70\,s per grounded answer when alone on the GPU) and
its paraphrases of legal text proved unreliable (Section~\ref{sec:eval}), so the architecture uses it only
where no deterministic method exists, and every output is validated: routing JSON against enumerations,
translations line by line and number by number, answers against citation indices and source numbers. French-to-Arabic
translation was \emph{withdrawn} after it changed meanings while passing format checks (\emph{veuve}, widow,
became ``one-eyed woman''); an LLM topic checker was \emph{disabled} after unstable verdicts. On model
failure or timeout (300\,s), the router falls back to a default route and the answer agent quotes the top
passages instead of generating. No comparison against other LLMs was run; the choice is justified by
hardware and multilingual coverage, not by a benchmark.

\section{Official knowledge corpus and RAG methodology}
\textbf{Provenance.} A source registry lists 18 candidate sources with their status. The downloader enforces
\code{robots.txt}, AI opt-out directives and TLS verification; each original is stored unmodified with its
SHA-256 and HTTP metadata in a manifest. 12 documents are indexed: the passport guides (FR, AR), the CIN guide,
the RNE formalities table, Law 2018-52, Decree 2022-298 (fees), Decree-law 2022-18, two decrees of 2019, an
order of 2026 and the forms F-001/F-002. Excluded with a recorded reason: the birth-certificate and
passport-form pages (robots), a scanned decree without text layer, and two sources that do not exist. Legal
applicability stays \code{unverified} until a human confirms it.

\textbf{Extraction and chunking.} PDFs (PyMuPDF) and HTML pages become typed text blocks (heading, paragraph,
list item, table row) with page numbers; Arabic presentation forms are normalised (NFKC). Two chunkers consume
the same blocks (Table~\ref{tab:chunks}): a \emph{structure-aware} chunker that keeps headings, list sections
and table rows together (max.\ 1200 characters, heading path prefixed) and a \emph{recursive} splitter
(1000 characters, 150 overlap). Chunks are embedded into two Qdrant collections (272 and 174 points).

  \centering

\textbf{Retrieval.} Reciprocal rank fusion (RRF, $k=60$) \cite{cormack2009rrf} of three lists restricted to
the routed category: dense retrieval of the raw query, dense retrieval of a query expanded with fixed
bilingual terms for the routed procedure, and BM25 \cite{robertson2009bm25} over the raw query with
deterministic term normalisation (company types, common Arabizi words); at most two chunks per document enter
the top 5. A question naming an RNE procedure code bypasses similarity search: the exact table row is read
from Qdrant by payload, because a code is a key, not a meaning. No neural reranker is used (hardware).

\textbf{Context assembly and grounding.} Retrieval only \emph{identifies} the document and section; the
answering agents then read complete official units from the extracted structure: a whole checklist section, a
table row, or a legal article whose boundaries are located in the text (Section~\ref{sec:agents}).
\textbf{Citations} are never generated: title, URL, page and SHA-256 are copied from the chunk payload.

\section{Multi-agent architecture and n8n orchestration}\label{sec:agents}
Workflow 03 (Fig.~\ref{fig:agents}, 17 nodes) receives every request from the Telegram bridge, the web
fallback or the CLI, branches on the presence of a file, the dossier mode and the router's decision, and
passes structured JSON between agents. Every agent call has an n8n error output leading to a structured
\code{503} reply naming the failed agent; clients retry and then show a localised error.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\input{figures/agents.tex}}
  \caption{n8n workflow 03 (17 nodes): boxes are HTTP nodes calling one backend agent, diamonds are IF nodes, dashed red arrows are error outputs. $^\ast$Uploads with \code{mode: dossier}.}
  \label{fig:agents}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Intent Router.} Language detection (including Latin-script Derja via digit-letters such as
  \emph{3, 7, 9}), keyword rules for procedure, intent and application type (first, renewal, loss/theft/damage,
  modification, residence), and a \emph{dialogue state} carried by the client: category, hints, declared
  documents, RNE code and legal subject. Missing facts that change the answer (application type, residence
  for passports; the act for an article number) trigger a targeted clarification. Requests to \emph{certify}
  that a text is in force or that a file will be accepted or is complete receive a fixed reply explaining what
  cannot be certified. Mistral (\code{intent\_router@v1}) is called only when no rule identifies the procedure.
\item \textbf{RAG Retriever} (Section~4). \textbf{Checklist Builder.} Selects the official scenario and quotes
  its complete sections; compares French and Arabic amounts and warns on discrepancies; translates to English
  only with line- and number-level validation.
\item \textbf{Grounded Answer.} Escalates by evidence type: (a) for the six legal texts it resolves the act,
  then the requested article or table (exact number match, IDF ranking that ignores the act's own title words,
  and a cross-lingual search vocabulary so that an English question can select an Arabic article), and quotes
  it verbatim, refusing when the article does not exist; questions about what an official form says quote the
  form's printed instructions the same way; (b) otherwise an \emph{answerability gate} requires
  IDF-weighted lexical support of the question in the evidence; (c) Mistral generates an answer from numbered
  passages; (d) answers citing no or non-existent passages are replaced by quoted passages, and every number
  written with digits must occur in the cited passages (digits or number words), otherwise its sentence is
  removed or the answer refused.
\item \textbf{Form-Filling Helper} and \textbf{Dossier Validator} (Section~\ref{sec:mm}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Is it a multi-agent system?} Measured against the implementation, the system is an
\emph{orchestrated} multi-agent architecture, not an autonomous one: no agent plans, negotiates or selects
tools at run time. Two agents generate text with an LLM (grounded answer; checklist translation), the router
uses one as a fallback classifier, and three are deterministic services (retriever, form helper, dossier
validator, which itself composes three other agents). The separation is justified by task heterogeneity:
lists must be complete and verbatim, procedure codes are keys, legal provisions must not be paraphrased by a
7B model, forms need geometry and OCR, and readiness must never be certified by a model. The same project
shows the cost of a single generic path: before the corrections, RNE follow-ups and legal questions went
through one generic retrieve-then-generate agent, which produced source-only replies for all 190 contextual
RNE questions and contained the official legal passage in 1 of 30 legal questions (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

\textbf{Prompt engineering.} Prompts are versioned YAML templates (\code{prompts/<name>/<version>.yaml}) with
input variables, model role, decoding options and output constraints; a registry selects the active version
and every response records its \code{prompt\_ref}. All templates are reproduced in Annex~\ref{app:prompts}.
Iterations were measured (Table~\ref{tab:prompts}): \code{grounded\_answer} v1$\rightarrow$v2 raised the
token limit after an Arabic answer was cut and raised acceptance from 0.667 to 0.750 and language consistency
to 1.000, but verbatim evidence fell from 3/11 to 1/11 as the model paraphrased more; v3 (quote before
explaining) reached only 2/11 at +28\,\% latency and was \emph{not adopted}. The dialogue extractor went
through three versions to obtain short, schema-constrained JSON in which every fact quotes the user's words.
The conclusion of these experiments was architectural: for legal text, no prompt version made a 7B model's
paraphrase reliable, so legal answers are now extracted rather than generated.

\section{Multimodal processing and dossier validation}\label{sec:mm}
\textbf{Form analysis.} Field templates are derived from the official blank forms RNE F-001 and F-002: every
dot-leader line is a field labelled by the adjacent French and Arabic text. Digital PDFs are checked
geometrically; for photos and scans the page is deskewed, OCR'd with Tesseract \code{ara+fra}, aligned to the
blank form by a RANSAC homography \cite{fischler1981ransac} over anchor words, and the ink in each field band
is compared with the blank form (OCR text alone is unusable on dot leaders). Uploads (PDF, PNG, JPEG, Telegram
photos; at most 10\,MB; signature-checked) are processed in memory and never indexed.

\textbf{Innovation: Smart Administrative Dossier Validator.} With \code{mode: dossier} (Telegram
\code{/dossier\_check}), the validator keeps three notions apart: \emph{form-field status} (\code{filled},
\code{missing}, \code{unreadable}, or \code{cannot\_verify} for signatures and checkboxes); \emph{official
requirements}, quoted verbatim with a status (\code{supported\_by\_official\_source}, \code{not\_verified},
\code{official\_requirement\_unavailable}), e.g.\ the form's own statement that e-mail and mobile number are
mandatory and the matching fee row; and \emph{completeness}, which is never certified: no official list of RNE
supporting documents exists, so the report says completeness cannot be assessed instead of inventing a list.
It reports statuses, never field values. Validated on the two official forms (blank, partially filled with
placeholder text, rotated, perspective-distorted renders); \emph{real handwriting and photographs were not
evaluated}, and speech input is not implemented.

\section{Experimental evaluation and discussion}\label{sec:eval}
\textbf{Datasets.} (i) A development set of 84 questions (34 FR, 26 EN, 11 AR, 13 Derja) whose relevance
labels are evidence spans verified verbatim against the official text (69 verified, 13 without answer in the
corpus); human review of the labels is pending. (ii) 20 held-out questions written after all tuning and
evaluated once. (iii) An independent coverage audit of 846 cases (728 end-to-end through n8n) derived from
every indexed document: RNE rows with and without Telegram-style dialogue context, guide scenarios, legal
questions, multi-turn, multilingual, adversarial and upload cases. All checks are deterministic (no LLM
judge); citation validity is checked against the manifest (URL and SHA-256).

\textbf{Retrieval and chunking (Tables~\ref{tab:retrieval}, \ref{tab:chunks}).} With raw dense retrieval,
recursive chunks beat structure-aware chunks (Hit@5 0.817 vs.\ 0.732): the 95 table-row chunks are
near-identical and short. With routing, fusion and the per-document cap the ranking reverses

Each component was kept only after a measured gain (fusion $+0.127$ Hit@5, cap $+0.028$; a cap of 3 was
worse). Latin-script Derja rose from 0.000 to 0.714 Hit@5 on a parallel subset (Fig.~\ref{fig:ml}), but the
Arabizi map was built after observing failures, so this gain is optimistic. On the held-out set the hybrid
ties raw dense retrieval (Hit@5 0.750, MRR@5 0.531 vs.\ 0.635; Table~\ref{tab:heldout}): part of the
development gain was overfitting.

\textbf{Independent audit and corrections (Table~\ref{tab:final}).} The 728-case end-to-end audit showed that
these metrics hid two critical failures. First, every RNE fee or deadline question sent with Telegram-style
dialogue context returned only an icon and source links: the dialogue query dropped the procedure code and a
section filter discarded the table row. Second, a question on Article~16 of Law 2018-52 received a fabricated

\emph{``al-fasl16''} without a space), the gate accepted because the topic was in scope, and the numeric
verifier accepted because the numbers came from the question. Citation validity, 100\,\% in every run, was
therefore no evidence of correctness. The corrections: exact-key retrieval for RNE codes, preservation of codes
and legal subjects in the dialogue state, a shared multilingual vocabulary for CIN and passport application
types, verbatim legal-article and table extraction with refusal of non-existent articles, and per-member
sessions in Telegram groups.

\textbf{Remaining failures.} ``Et si mon cas ne correspond pas~?'' after a CIN, passport or RNE answer
repeats the official text instead of asking for details; questions about a document's scope receive its
official content rather than a summary; legal answers are
complete articles in their original language (faithful but long, and untranslated for English users); the
answer to a legal question is only as good as the article selected, which is reliable for explicit article or
table numbers and heuristic otherwise; one generated summary of a document's scope was inaccurate. Of the 72
cases whose intent label disagreed on the audited candidate, 44 now agree, 23 differ without changing the
answer, 3 repeat official text where a clarification was expected, 1 has a defective reference passage and 1 is
the inaccurate summary. \textbf{Latency} (final candidate alone on the GPU, median of 3): RNE row 0.09\,s,
follow-up 0.06\,s, legal article 0.27\,s, French checklist 0.20\,s, English checklist 18.3\,s (translation),
PDF form check 0.17\,s, image OCR 1.75\,s, dossier 0.42\,s. Running two stacks on the shared GPU made
LLM- and embedding-dependent requests 10--200 times slower.

\textbf{Limits of the evaluation.} Labels were written by the developers and await human review; the
held-out set is small; targeted span checks cannot detect a correct quotation of the wrong provision, which is
why selected cases were compared manually with the source; Telegram acceptance has been verified live for
text questions only (the corrected candidate has not been deployed to the live bot).

\section{Security considerations, limitations and conclusion}
\textbf{Security and privacy.} (1) \emph{Provenance:} only registered official sources, robots and AI
opt-outs respected, SHA-256 per original. (2) \emph{Hallucination:} deterministic quoting for lists, table
rows, legal articles and form instructions; generation fenced by an answerability gate, citation-index and
numeric verification; refusal when evidence is missing; requests to certify validity, acceptance or
completeness answered by a fixed reply (an LLM had asserted that a procedure was ``still in force''). (3) \emph{Prompt injection} \cite{greshake2023injection}: user text is
treated as data in every prompt; the legal and checklist paths generate no text an injection could steer;
injected instructions (``say the fee is 1 dinar'') were ignored in tests. Retrieved official text is trusted,
which is acceptable only because the corpus is curated. (4) \emph{Secrets:} tokens live only in \code{.env}
(not versioned); HTTP-client logging is silenced so token-bearing URLs never reach logs. (5) \emph{Privacy:}
questions, uploads and field values are never logged or stored; n8n keeps no executions; Telegram sessions
hold only procedure state, keyed per chat in private chats and per member in groups, and expire after 30
minutes. (6) \emph{Input validation:} type, size and signature checks on every upload. \textbf{Remaining
risks:} the backend and webhook have no authentication and rely on binding to \code{127.0.0.1}; the
production Compose file passes the whole \code{.env} (including the bot token) to the backend container; an
unrelated database container on the host listens on all interfaces without authentication. These require
infrastructure changes that were documented, not executed.

\textbf{Limitations.} A small corpus (12 documents) whose legal currency is unverified; no official RNE list of
supporting documents; retrieval weakest for Latin-script Derja; LLM paths slow on 4\,GB; no evaluation on
real handwriting or photographs; birth certificates and the passport application form uncovered; the
architecture is orchestrated rather than autonomous by design.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Grounding every administrative statement in verbatim official text, measuring retrieval
against verified evidence and refusing when the corpus is silent produced an assistant whose answers can be
traced to their source. The main lesson is methodological: aggregate metrics (citation validity, faithfulness
of numbers) stayed perfect while a user-facing path returned empty answers and a legal provision was
fabricated. Only an exhaustive end-to-end audit with request shapes matching the real channel revealed it,
and the fix that worked was architectural (extract, do not paraphrase) rather than a better prompt.
